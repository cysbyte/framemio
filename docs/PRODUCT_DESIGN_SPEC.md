# FrameMio Product Design Specification

## Target User

People who capture life on iPhone every day but rarely have the time, confidence, or energy to turn that media into something meaningful. Core audiences include travelers, couples, families, pet owners, and people who want social-media-ready memory videos without becoming video editors.

## Core User Problem

Photos and videos contain the raw material of a memory, but the important moments are buried in the Camera Roll. Turning them into a coherent story usually means sorting, trimming, choosing music, and learning a timeline editor. The effort prevents people from making anything at all.

## Product Promise

FrameMio understands the moments already captured on iPhone, identifies what matters, and automatically creates a beautiful short memory video with almost no decisions required.

## Emotional Value

FrameMio helps people revisit how a moment felt, not just what happened. It creates delight, recognition, nostalgia, and the feeling that everyday life is worth keeping and sharing.

## Primary User Journey

1. **Create Story** — Start from a simple, clear invitation to make something beautiful.
2. **Choose a context** — Select Today, Weekend, Trip, or another meaningful moment type.
3. **Select photos/videos** — Pick a lightweight set of media from the iPhone Camera Roll.
4. **Understand moments** — The system quietly interprets time, place, people, scenes, motion, and audio.
5. **Create and automatically edit** — Story intelligence determines the narrative, pacing, and visual sequence.
6. **Preview generated story** — Watch the result in an immersive, calm presentation.
7. **Save/share** — Keep the finished memory or share it naturally from iOS.

## Visual Personality

Premium, warm, cinematic, calm, intimate, photography-first, and unmistakably native to iOS. The interface should feel like a quiet gallery: generous space, soft surfaces, restrained motion, rich media, and typography that supports the story without becoming the story.

## Design Principles

- Make the user’s media the visual hero.
- Remove decisions before adding controls.
- Reveal intelligence through results, not technical explanations.
- Use clear hierarchy, one primary action, and calm transitions.
- Prefer familiar iOS interaction patterns and native gestures.
- Make the experience feel personal without feeling sentimental or theatrical.
- Treat automatic editing as the product, not as a hidden timeline editor.

## Explicitly Avoid

- Timeline editors, tracks, clip trimming, and professional editing vocabulary.
- CapCut, Premiere, Final Cut, or desktop video-editing visual language.
- Dense dashboards, analytics, settings-first navigation, or generic AI SaaS styling.
- Toolbars that compete with photos and videos.
- Endless configuration of music, transitions, filters, templates, or effects.
- A generic slideshow generator that simply orders media chronologically.
